## Statistical break checks

The training-only CUSUM diagnostic and rise/fall slope diagnostic are saved in `reports/structural/training_diagnostics.json`. Their fixed specification is one-observation NYH proxy changes with the Part 3 14-calendar-day availability assumption. CUSUM examines residual instability; it does not identify a legal date, establish causality or license a full-sample optimized split. Small samples, irregular event spacing, heteroskedasticity and dependent residuals limit nominal p-values. No diagnostic derived from final-test outcomes is fed back into model choice.

An error-correction model needs an economically supported long-run relationship and compatible integration behavior. Current samples have irregular decision spacing, gaps and incomplete official pricing FX. The gate refuses to treat that event sequence as 60 consecutive weekly observations. No interpolation, synthetic weekly prices or unsubstantiated cointegration claim is used. Long-run ECM estimation remains blocked by these data requirements; the implemented diagnostic can screen a later admissible sample.

## Interpretation

The two statistical segments are research stratification, not a complete taxonomy of legal regimes. Both receive separate coefficients, validation choices, calibration histories and metrics. Later fiscal periods and crisis periods are reported as excluded, never pooled merely to increase sample size. More frequent policy decisions can change event-lag meaning; all tested market windows explicitly use observation counts and calendar-day offsets, rather than calling an event a day.
